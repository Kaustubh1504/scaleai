# prob-002 rubric — Lightweight Load Balancer

Score each category 1–4. **4** = strong hire signal, **3** = hire, **2** = lean no, **1** = no.
Descriptions of 3 and 1 interpolate between the two given. Write down evidence, not impressions.

| Category | 4 looks like (this problem) | 2 looks like (this problem) |
|---|---|---|
| **Correct execution** | Parts 1 and 2 pass the hidden tests, the 80% change included, and Part 3 passes or nearly does. The rotation rule after removals and failures, the cooldown boundary, "more than" staleness and the cross-drain attempt budget are right without prompting. | Happy-path failover works, but the rotation skips or repeats workers after a removal, `TaskFailed` is retried on other workers, cooldown never expires in `worker_state()`, or a stuck task is lost or reordered by `drain()`. |
| **Debugging & unblocking** | When a rotation test fails, prints the sequence of `(worker, outcome)` and finds the off-by-one. Uses `go_silent()` / `clock.time()` to see that a timeout moved the clock and reasons from there. Reads the `MockWorker` source when unsure of its behaviour. | Tweaks the index arithmetic until a test passes; needs hint 2+ to notice that a silent worker advances the fake clock or that killed workers send no heartbeats; stuck for more than 5 minutes on a heap `TypeError`. |
| **Systematic thinking** | Reads the repo first and names the starter's problems (overwrite, silent unregister, `% len` after removal) before coding. Separates membership, per-worker health and routing. Writes one failover loop with an explicit exception-to-state mapping. The 80% change is a one-line predicate edit. `drain()` reuses dispatch with a budget instead of duplicating the loop. | Starts editing `dispatch()` immediately. State lives in several parallel dicts or on the worker object. The overload threshold is copy-pasted into several places. `drain()` re-implements routing, or wraps `dispatch()` in a `while True` with ad-hoc counters. |
| **Production quality** | Injected clock everywhere. Validates heartbeat payloads (no `KeyError`/`ZeroDivisionError` from a bad one). Logs state transitions. Never reads `worker.state`. No bare `except` (unexpected errors propagate). Uses `from None` / clear messages on `KeyError`/`ValueError`. Doesn't lose a queued task on any exception. Says the balancer is single-threaded or adds a lock deliberately. | `time.time()`; `except Exception: continue` that swallows real bugs; trusts `payload["capacity"]`; peeks at `MockWorker.alive`; mutates the registry while iterating it. |
| **Testing** | Tests alongside each rule with `FakeClock` + `MockWorker`: dead, silent, slow, full, crash-mid-task, removal mid-rotation, cooldown boundary, staleness boundary, `WorkerFleet`-driven recovery, priority/FIFO, budget across two drains. Keeps the starter tests green. Updates their own tests for the 80% change. | One or two happy-path tests, written at the end; no test drives a worker into failure; starter tests broken and not noticed. |
| **End-to-end ownership** | Runs `python -m lb.simulate` before and after, and makes the demo use the new queue (or says how). Points out the duplicate `label-002` in `data/tasks.jsonl` against the new `submit()` rule. Closes with what is done, what isn't, and the known risks (duplicate execution on timeout). | Never runs the demo. Leaves `NotImplementedError` stubs or TODOs unexplained. Stops when "my test passes" without checking the existing tests or the demo. |
| **Communication** | Explains the rotation rule back in their own words and checks it against the PART1 example table. Asks sharp questions the spec leaves to judgement (should overload rejections count toward `max_attempts`? they do, as specified, and it's worth debating). Explains why a timeout is ambiguous. | Silent for long stretches, or asks questions PART files answer explicitly (e.g. "does a dead worker come back in Part 1?"); can't explain why `TaskFailed` shouldn't be retried. |
