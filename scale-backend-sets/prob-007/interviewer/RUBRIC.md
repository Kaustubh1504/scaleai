# prob-007 rubric — Worker Pool with Failover and DLQ

Score each category 1–4. **4** = strong hire signal, **3** = hire, **2** = lean no, **1** = no.
Descriptions of 3 and 1 interpolate between the two given. Write down evidence, not impressions.

| Category | 4 looks like (this problem) | 2 looks like (this problem) |
|---|---|---|
| **Correct execution** | Parts 1 and 2 pass the hidden tests, the pinned-job change included, and Part 3 passes or nearly does. The tie-break on earlier calls, front-of-queue order with two failing workers, "down until the next heartbeat", backoff measured after the call, and "two *different* workers" for poison are right without prompting. | The round runs, but single jobs always land on `w1`, failed-over jobs go to the back or are re-sent to the dead worker in the same round, `TaskFailedError` marks the worker down, or a job in backoff blocks the queue or is dead-lettered by `drain()`. |
| **Debugging & unblocking** | When the crash example fails, prints `(job, worker, outcome)` per send and `pending()` per round, and finds the cause quickly. Notices that `go_silent()` moved the fake clock by `job_timeout_s` and that this can make other workers stale. Reads the `MockWorker` source to see that a killed worker never records `received`. | Changes the requeue order until a test passes; needs hint 2+ to see that a dead worker raises `WorkerUnreachableError` before admitting the job, or that `WorkerFleet.run()` advances the clock; stuck for more than 5 minutes on why a backoff job never becomes eligible (never moved the fake clock). |
| **Systematic thinking** | Writes job and worker records first. Separates plan from run, and rebuilds the queue once per round. One `min()` with a named key; one predicate for "can this worker take this job" (the pin is a one-line addition); one exception-to-outcome mapping. Classifies errors as the job's fault versus the worker's fault before writing Part 3. | One long `tick()` that mutates the queue while iterating it. State lives in parallel dicts keyed by id. Liveness is a background-style `check()` that has to be called in the right places. Retry and failover share an ad-hoc counter, so the budget rules leak into each other. |
| **Production quality** | Injected clock and no sleeps. Copies of the payload on submit and on every read path. Validates job ids and heartbeat payloads (no `KeyError` from a bad one). Logs worker down/up and dead-letter transitions with reasons. Unexpected exceptions propagate (no bare `except`). Never reads `worker.state`/`alive`/`in_flight`. Says the pool is single-threaded, and how it would become concurrent. | `time.time()`/`time.sleep()`; `except Exception: continue` that swallows bugs; returns internal lists from `dead_letters`/`results`; reads `MockWorker.alive` to skip dead workers; the DLQ entry has no history, so an operator can't tell why a job died. |
| **Testing** | Tests alongside each rule with `FakeClock`, `MockWorker` and `CrashingWorker`: the PART1 and PART2 example tables, tie-breaks, slot limit, idempotent submit, crash/kill/silent/slow/overloaded, the staleness boundary, rejoin, the backoff schedule with `clock.set`, `max_attempts`, poison (same vs different workers), and drain. Keeps the starter tests green and updates them for the pin. | A couple of happy-path tests written at the end; no test drives a worker failure or moves the clock; never checks `pending()` order. |
| **End-to-end ownership** | Runs `python -m pool.demo` and explains its output: malformed lines skipped, the duplicate `resize-001` ignored, the conflicting `resize-002` rejected, `poison-001` dead-lettered after killing two workers, `broken-001` after 3 attempts, `flaky-001` done on its third try, workers rejoining after the supervisor restarts them. Closes with what is done, what isn't, and the at-least-once caveat. | Never runs the demo or reads `data/jobs.jsonl`. Leaves `NotImplementedError` stubs unexplained. Stops at "my test passes". |
| **Communication** | Explains the round model back in their own words and checks the example tables against it. Asks sharp questions the spec leaves to judgement (should overload refusals ever give up? should a resubmit with a different pin be a conflict?). Explains why a timeout makes execution at-least-once. | Silent for long stretches, or asks questions the PART files answer explicitly (e.g. "do failovers count toward max_attempts?"); can't say why a poison job shouldn't be retried a third time. |
